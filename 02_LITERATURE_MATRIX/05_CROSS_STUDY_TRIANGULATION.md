# Cross-Study Triangulation

## Studies

### Study A — NASA OSD-871
**Model:** Human iPSC-derived dopaminergic organoids

**Exposure:** Real spaceflight on the International Space Station in low-Earth orbit

**Primary readout:** Bulk RNA-seq / transcriptional profiling

**Key maturation-related result:**  
LEO-associated samples showed a transcriptional pattern broadly consistent with maturation. TH and DDC increased, while selected progenitor and cell-cycle-associated markers decreased.

**Key identity/integrity result from this Iterate:**  
The preregistered overall Dopaminergic Identity / Integrity score was higher in LEO than Ground. However, this was not universal across subgroups: Control + microglia showed increased maturation but decreased dopaminergic identity.

**Key stress/glial result from this Iterate:**  
The overall Stress score was lower in LEO, but subgroup sensitivity analysis showed that the Control without microglia stress result was fragile to removal of several stress genes.

## Study B — Park et al. 2026

**Model:**  
Human cerebral organoids

**Exposure:**  
Ground-based 3D clinostat-based simulated microgravity

**Experimental conditions:**  
- N30: 30 days normal gravity
- C30: 30 days continuous clinostat exposure
- C15 N15: 15 days clinostat exposure followed by 15 days normal gravity

**Primary readouts:**  
Targeted gene-expression analysis, selected protein measurements, and neurochemical / neurotransmitter profiling.

**Key progenitor / developmental result:**  
SOX2 and other pluripotency-associated transcripts decreased strongly under continuous clinostat exposure.

**Key neuronal maturation result:**  
Several neuronal differentiation / maturation-associated transcripts, including MAP2, DCX and VGLUT1, also decreased strongly. TUBB3 increased, showing that the neuronal response was not uniform.

**Key regional-identity result:**  
Multiple regional specification genes, including FOXG1, DLX2, NKX2.1 and SIX6, were strongly reduced.

**Key functional / neurochemical result:**  
Dopamine decreased by approximately 53% under continuous clinostat exposure. Acetylcholine, norepinephrine and glutamate also decreased, while GABA increased.

**Key glial result:**  
GFAP increased strongly, particularly after the re-adaptation condition, suggesting altered astroglial state / lineage bias.

**Important transcript–protein discrepancy:**  
Despite strong RNA-level changes in SOX2 and MAP2, their measured protein levels were not significantly altered.

**Main interpretation:**  
Reduced progenitor / stemness-associated transcription did not correspond to straightforward beneficial neuronal maturation. Loss of SOX2 occurred alongside reduced neuronal differentiation markers, disrupted regional identity and lower neurotransmitter levels.

**Critical limitation for comparison with OSD-871:**  
This study used cerebral organoids and a ground-based 3D clinostat. It therefore cannot be treated as a direct replication of the dopaminergic organoid ISS / LEO experiment.
## Critical Model Differences

| Feature | NASA OSD-871 | Park et al. 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Organoid type | Human iPSC-derived dopaminergic organoids | Human cerebral organoids |
| Biological focus | Dopaminergic / midbrain-relevant biology | Broader cerebral / forebrain-like development |
| Gravity model | Actual ISS / low-Earth-orbit spaceflight | Ground-based 3D clinostat simulated microgravity |
| Exposure environment | Full spaceflight environment | Simulated altered-gravity environment on Earth |
| Dopaminergic specificity | High | Low / not designed as a dopaminergic model |
| Main molecular readout | Bulk RNA-seq | Targeted gene-expression measurements |
| Protein-level measurements | Not part of this reproduction | Selected proteins measured |
| Neurochemical / neurotransmitter measurements | Not part of this reproduction | Available |
| Direct functional maturation evidence | Limited | Partial biochemical evidence, but not equivalent to electrophysiological validation |
| Directly comparable models? | No | No |

## Convergent Evidence

- Both studies show that altered-gravity / spaceflight-associated conditions can substantially change neural-organoid transcriptional state.
- Both show changes in progenitor / developmental programs.
- Both demonstrate that interpretation based on a small number of markers can be misleading.
- Both support the idea that maturation should be evaluated together with broader identity and state information.

## Divergent Evidence

- NASA OSD-871 shows an overall transcriptional pattern broadly consistent with increased neuronal maturation and, in most matched subgroups, preserved or increased dopaminergic identity.
- Park et al. 2026 show that reduced progenitor / stemness-associated expression can coexist with lower neuronal differentiation markers, reduced neurotransmitter levels and increased GFAP.
- The direction and meaning of altered-gravity responses therefore appear strongly dependent on organoid type, exposure model and biological readout.
- The two studies should not be treated as direct replications of one another.

## What This Means for the Maturation-versus-Integrity Hypothesis

Reduced progenitor-associated transcription is not sufficient evidence of useful neuronal maturation.

A stronger interpretation requires simultaneous evaluation of:

1. neuronal maturation,
2. regional / lineage identity,
3. dopaminergic identity where relevant,
4. progenitor / cell-cycle state,
5. stress and glial-state changes,
6. and ideally protein-level or functional outputs.

The OSD-871 Iterate results are consistent with an overall maturation-associated LEO shift, but this interpretation is not universal across all subgroups. In particular, the Control + microglia condition showed increased maturation together with decreased dopaminergic identity.

The Park et al. 2026 study further demonstrates that loss of progenitor / stemness-associated signal can occur alongside weaker neuronal differentiation and lower neurotransmitter output.

Therefore, “more mature” and “more biologically useful as a disease model” should be treated as separate hypotheses.



## Key Triangulation Insight

Park et al. (2026) showed that reduced progenitor/stemness-associated expression under clinostat-based simulated microgravity did not correspond to straightforward beneficial maturation. SOX2 decreased, but neuronal differentiation markers including MAP2, DCX and VGLUT1 also decreased, dopamine levels were reduced, and GFAP increased. This supports the need to evaluate maturation together with neuronal identity, functional neurotransmitter output and stress/lineage changes rather than interpreting progenitor-marker loss alone as beneficial maturation.
